**To level liquid screed over underfloor heating, you push levellers into the insulation board, roughly one every 2 m², mark the datum level with a laser, adjust the ring of each leveller to that line and pour the screed until it covers the ring.** With the [KREOX PIKO](/en/screed-levellers/piko/) the whole process is done without metal tripods, using a leveller of about 20 grams that does not rust and can be reused.

## What you need

- **[PIKO](/en/screed-levellers/piko/) levellers**: sold in boxes of 100 units.
- **A laser level**: a standard one or one with a receiver will do.
- **Liquid screed**, following its manufacturer’s instructions.

## Step 1 · Push the PIKO into the insulation board

The PIKO has a tip that goes into the underfloor heating insulation board without tools. Spread them over the whole surface, roughly **one every 2 m²**, so you have reference points across the room.

## Step 2 · Mark the datum level with a laser

Set up the laser and mark the height at which the screed surface should finish. That line is the reference for all the levellers.

## Step 3 · Adjust the ring on each PIKO

Slide the ring on each PIKO up to the laser line. The ring is also a **visual reference** during pouring: the screed has to reach exactly up to it.

## Step 4 · Pour the liquid screed

Pour the screed until it reaches the height of the rings. Then remove the PIKO and clean them for the next job.

## Why not use metal tripods

A metal tripod weighs about 1 kg, corrodes with the alkaline pH of the screed and all three legs must be firmly seated. For 100 m², that is about 50 kg of tripods versus 1 kg of PIKO. See the full comparison in [PIKO versus the tripod](/en/screed-levellers/piko-vs-metal-tripod/).

## After pouring

PIKO levellers do not rust: they are cleaned with water and reused. If the washer wears with use, only that part is replaced with the [spare parts](/en/screed-levellers/spare-parts/).
